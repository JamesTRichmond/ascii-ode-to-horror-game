# Stanza → chapter narrative map

Chapter *N* is built from Stanza *N* of Arthur O'Shaughnessy's *Ode* (public domain, 1873).
Each row names the lines that generate the chapter's place, threat and rule. "Rule" is the one
mechanic that exists only in that chapter.

| # | Chapter | Key lines | Place | Threat | Chapter rule | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lone Sea-Breakers** | "Wandering by lone sea-breakers / And sitting by desolate streams" · "On whom the pale moon gleams" | Moonlit shingle shore, a dead stream running inland | **Forsakers** — pale drifters that walk toward sound | Tutorial: you are mute until the first measure; humming reveals what moonlight does not | 3 measures, then the stream-mouth gate |
| 2 | **The Deathless Ditty** | "With wonderful deathless ditties / We build up the world's great cities" · "One man with a dream… shall conquer a crown / And three… can trample a kingdom down" | A city that is not there until sung | **The Crowned** (one relentless stalker); after the third measure, **three Tramplers** | Humming beside a foundation stone raises or lowers its walls — you build your own route and your own barricades | 3 measures, then outrun the trampling to the gate |
| 3 | **Nineveh and Babel** | "Built Nineveh with our sighing / And Babel itself in our mirth" · "each age is a dream that is dying / Or one that is coming to birth" | Buried twin ruins | **Sighs** — drifting fog bodies that drain Breath; **Mirths** — fast, loud, only in the Babel state | The map alternates between a *dying* layout and a *being-born* layout on a fixed period; walls exist in one, the other, or both | 4 measures split across both states |
| 4 | **One Breath** | "A breath of our inspiration / Is the life of each generation" · "The soldier, the king, and the peasant / Are working together in one" | A generation hall of hanging husks | **The Three-in-One** — a single fused body that hunts by hearing your breathing | Husks animate while you breathe near them; holding breath is the only way past, and Breath does not regenerate while held | 3 measures, exit past the Three-in-One |
| 5 | **The Goodly House** | "They had no vision amazing / Of the goodly house they are raising" · "A light that doth not depart" · "Wrought flame in another man's heart" | A vast unlit house under construction | **Blind Builders** — sightless, they path to noise | You carry a flame. Lighting a brazier makes that room safe and permanently lit (radiance-cascade showcase); the flame gutters if you hum | Light all braziers; the last one opens the door |
| 6 | **The Multitudes** | "And the multitudes are enlisted / In the faith that their fathers resisted" · "The dream that was scorned yesterday" | A processional avenue | **The Multitude** — a marching crowd, harmless while you keep its pace and silence | Moving against the flow, or any voice at all, marks you as a scorner and the crowd turns | 3 measures taken from inside the march, then the far arch |
| 7 | **A Little Apart** | "Ceaseless and sorrowless we! / The glory about us clinging" · "we dwell… A little apart from ye" | The Choir's bright cloister, overlaid on the world of men | **Choristers** — luminous, only solid in the Apart layer | Humming shifts you between *Among* and *Apart*. Each layer has different walls; measures are in one, doors in the other | 4 measures, exit only reachable Among |
| 8 | **The Infinite Morning** | "we are afar with the dawning / And the suns that are not yet high" · "already goes forth the warning / That ye of the past must die" | An open plain toward sunrise | **The Dawn** itself — an advancing front of light | Inversion of every chapter so far: light kills. A warning cry sounds before each surge; stand in shadow or be unwritten | Reach the far shade before the sun is high |
| 9 | **The Dazzling Unknown Shore** | "Great hail! we cry to the comers / From the dazzling unknown shore" · "in spite of a dreamer who slumbers / And a singer who sings no more" | The Chapter 1 shore, renewed and too bright | **The Comers**, and **the Slumberer** — your own shape, asleep at the water | Final choice made by play, not by menu: hum the full song at the Slumberer (it wakes; you are taken into the Choir) or cross the beach in held breath (the world renews without you) | Either ending |

## Arc

| Act | Chapters | Movement | What the player learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — The Makers | 1–3 | Finding a voice | The world is made by song; song is dangerous to have |
| II — The Made | 4–6 | Using the voice on others | People are material to the Choir; your light makes more of them |
| III — Apart | 7–9 | Refusing or returning | You were one of them; the last stanza is about you |

## Difficulty and pacing intent

| Chapter | Tension curve | New pressure | Relief |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low, rising | Sound attracts | Slow threat, open ground |
| 2 | Medium, spike at end | Pursuit | You can wall pursuers out |
| 3 | Medium | Shifting layout, Breath drain | Predictable period |
| 4 | High | Cannot regenerate while hidden | Small map |
| 5 | Medium, release | Darkness | Every brazier is permanent safety |
| 6 | High, sustained | Crowd, no voice | Clear rule |
| 7 | Medium | Two layouts to hold in mind | Threat is avoidable by layer |
| 8 | Very high | Timer, light is lethal | Short |
| 9 | Low, then the choice | None new | Ending |

## Text delivery

Each chapter opens with its stanza set as an inscription, one line at a time. Lines recur
diegetically inside the chapter as wall inscriptions written in the rune dictionary. The poem is the
only narration; there is no other dialogue.
